# Bulk iron-based superconductors: literature review, 2024 to October 2026
### Focus: attempts to push bulk Tc past the ~55–58 K record

Compiled by a QUBIT Lab sub-agent. Sources: the arXiv API (metadata, abstracts and DOIs pulled programmatically), the arXiv HTML of the 2026 review arXiv:2607.11355, nature.com, APS and IOP pages, and web search.
**Identifier policy.** A DOI marked (v) was checked against the arXiv metadata or the publisher page during this session. A DOI marked (m) is from memory and was not checked; treat it as "identifier not verified". If no DOI is given, use the arXiv ID.

**Between 2024 and October 2026 I found no peer-reviewed or credible preprint report of a bulk Fe-based superconductor above 58.6 K, at ambient pressure or under pressure.** The 2026 review arXiv:2607.11355 (v2) puts the ambient-pressure maximum of 1111 at "~56 K" and gives ~52 K and ~55 K for the FeSe-derived SC-II phases as "the highest reported transition temperatures for bulk FeCh-based superconductors."

**Onset vs zero resistance.** Sm1111 polycrystals usually have narrow transitions (ΔT ~1–3 K), so the onset and zero-resistance values are close. Claims of 57–58.6 K are onset or midpoint resistive values; the χ onsets are usually 1–3 K lower. No 1111 report in 2024–2026 claims zero resistance above 56–57 K.

## 5. Theory on Tc ceilings and predictors (2025–2026)

| Work | Content and numbers | ID |
|---|---|---|
| Qin & Yang, "Intrinsic constraint on Tc for unconventional superconductivity" | Static auxiliary-field MC on t–J-type and Hubbard pairing models gives an intrinsic maximum **Tc/J ≈ 0.04–0.07**, where J is the pairing (exchange) scale. They argue that cuprates, **iron-based**, nickelates and heavy fermions "almost exhaust" the Tc their J allows. Room-temperature Tc would need J ≳ 400–700 meV. | npj Quantum Mater. 10, 13 (2025) doi:10.1038/s41535-025-00733-y (v) |
| Review: "Iron-Based Superconductors: A Decade of Materials, Magnetism, and Mechanisms" | Restates the empirical predictors: Tc is highest at the regular-tetrahedron As–Fe–As angle **α ≈ 109.47°** and at anion height **h ≈ 1.38 Å** (Kuroki: dxy band position). | LaFeAsO by DFT+DMFT with vertex (ladder) | d-wave and s± compete closely. s± leads in ladder DMFT. No Tc number. | arXiv:2604.18582 (2026) |

None of these papers predicts a bulk Fe-based Tc above ~60 K. The only quantitative ceilings available (Tc/J ≲ 0.07, and the empirical α and h optimum, which the Sm1111 record already sits near) both imply the known families are close to saturation.

## 7. Assessment: is there an evidence-backed route to a BULK Fe-based compound above 100 K at ambient pressure?

**No.** The evidence as of October 2026:

1. **The ambient bulk record has not moved in about 9 years** (58.6 K, 2017; ~55 K since 2008). Every 2024–2026 high-pressure-synthesis study (Sm1111 at 4 GPa: 57 K; Pr1111: 52.3 K; 1144 HP-HTS) lands at or below the old values.
2. **Doping saturates.** H− doping extends the electron-doping range to x ~0.5 without raising the Sm ceiling. SC1 and SC2 merge at ~55 K. Over-hydrogenation (LaFeSiH1.6) kills SC.
3. **Pressure does not help 1111 and never exceeds ~55 K in any Fe compound.** The SC-II phases (48–55 K) need 5–12 GPa, and nobody has shown they survive decompression. Pressure-quench has retained only 37 K, in FeSe.
4. **The best 2026 lead, (THA)xFeSe, peaks at ~55 K onset but only ~33 K zero resistance and ~42–44 K diamagnetic onset.** Monolayer-like 2D fluctuations limit phase coherence. The extra Tc in monolayer FeSe/STO (~65 K gap) appears to need interfacial phonons, which a bulk intercalate does not have.
5. **Theory gives ceilings, not roads.** Tc/J ≲ 0.07 implies the Fe-based families are near their exchange-limited maximum. Ab initio spin-fluctuation calculations underestimate the existing Tcs, and the only quantitative predictions (FeSeH >40 K, ThFeAsN 22 K) fall well below 100 K.
6. **The only >77 K bulk claim (Fe-Se-H, room temperature, from EPR) lacks basic evidence and has not been reproduced.**

Speculative directions, none with evidence beyond ~60 K:
- Pressure-quenching the SC-II phase of Lix(NH3)yFe2Se2 or (Li,Fe)OHFeSe. The target would be ~50–55 K at ambient, which at best ties the record.
- Bulk artificial superlattices that reproduce the FeSe/SrTiO3 interface (an FeSe/oxide heterostructure). This might reach ~60–70 K.
- Cleaner, low-disorder electron-doped FeSe intercalates, given the Tc–ρ0 scaling.

A realistic upper target from current evidence is ~60–70 K. **100 K in a bulk Fe-based compound at ambient pressure has no experimental or quantitative theoretical support.**
