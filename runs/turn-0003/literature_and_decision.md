# Literature and decision record — checked 2026-10-10

## Primary sources actually retrieved

1. **Dolui et al., Feasible Route to High-Temperature Ambient-Pressure Hydride Superconductivity**, Physical Review Letters 132, 166001 (2024), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevLett.132.166001`. Full publisher PDF retrieved using the APS harvest endpoint and read with pypdf. Predicts cubic Mg2IrH6 at ambient pressure, anisotropic Migdal–Eliashberg Tc 160–175 K. Reports a=6.66 A and H 24e x=0.26. The prose appears to reverse the metal-site labels: stoichiometry requires Mg on multiplicity-8 and Ir on multiplicity-4 sites. Used corrected assignment, checked against source 2. The predicted H7 precursor route and small short-time hydrogen mobility do not establish retained ambient H6 or macroscopic sample lifetime. Original source discusses metastability and competing structures; this turn does not recompute a hull.

2. **Hansen et al., Synthesis of Mg2IrH5: A potential pathway to high-Tc hydride superconductivity at ambient pressure**, `arXiv:2406.09538v1`. Retrieved abstract and full HTML (arXiv submission date June 13, 2024; HTML rendering date August 24, 2026 is NOT a new publication date). Experiments up to about 28 GPa are consistent with H5, not H6. Metal-lattice diffraction does not reliably establish H content; vibrational evidence matters. Describes disordered IrH5 units with average H occupancy 5/6 and possible configurational stabilization. The paragraph near the conclusion reports independently reproduced dense-grid Tc values near 90–120 K with methodological dependence. This is the direct basis for the 105 K midpoint used here; neither a fresh calculation nor a statistical posterior. Authors discuss insertion as a possible non-equilibrium route, not a demonstrated synthesis of H6.

3. **Sinha et al., High-pressure stabilization of Mg2IrH7: Structural proximity to high-Tc superconductivity**, `arXiv:2602.23675v1`, submitted February 27, 2026. Retrieved primary abstract. Assigns cubic H7 above about 40 GPa with a related hexagonal hydride; reports insulating transport and reversion to cubic H5 around 20 GPa on room-temperature decompression. Associated DOI `10.1103/zjbx-2wxp`; publisher-deposited Crossref record has accepted and published date fields September 28, 2026. Publisher webpage was blocked (403), so no independent assertion about changes in its revised abstract is retained. This is negative evidence against simply decompressing H7 at room temperature to obtain H6, not proof that every non-equilibrium route fails.

4. **Superconducting Hydride Mg2RhH6 Experimentally Achieved at Lower Pressure**, JACS, DOI `10.1021/jacs.6c08889`, publisher-deposited date August 20, 2026. Retrieved Crossref publisher-deposited abstract; full publisher text blocked. Reports precursor H5 hydrogenation, superconducting resistive transition at 24 K/30 GPa and 29 K/53 GPa, zero resistance and magnetic-field suppression. This supports the broad complex-hydride approach only: 30 GPa is NOT ambient pressure, Rh is not Ir, and no ambient-pressure Ir-H6 validation follows.

## Scope and omissions
This is a targeted source check, not an exhaustive literature census. No original DFT, electron–phonon calculation, phonon stability analysis, finite-temperature phase diagram, lifetime calculation or sample measurement was performed. Older/independent Tc values cannot be combined as statistically independent measurements. A provisional independent 77 K datum was removed from inputs because its full primary table was not checked. We do not assign a probability that a synthesis succeeds or an unconditional expected Tc.

## Proposed candidate and fail conditions
**Mg2IrH6, cubic Fm-3m, stoichiometric fully occupied IrH6 units, ambient external pressure. Conditional forecast 105 K, low realization confidence.** This is QUBIT's adoption of an existing theoretical candidate, not a novel composition or discovery. It is not a room-temperature forecast.

A staged specialist-laboratory test should first establish retained H6 rather than H5 or H7 after a controlled non-equilibrium treatment. Do not specify unvalidated synthesis temperatures/rates as a recipe. Seek local vibrational signatures and quantitative H/metal stoichiometry; deuterated neutron diffraction, if feasible, can support site occupancy but isotope effects need separate treatment. An average H content of six can also describe an H5/H7 mixture, so composition and phase-resolved evidence must agree. Then test the same identified ambient-pressure phase for zero resistance plus a bulk diamagnetic response and, where feasible, a heat-capacity anomaly, with field dependence, repeated samples, pressure verification and aging checks. A transport drop alone is insufficient. Failure to retain H6 leaves the Tc forecast untested; verified pure H6 without superconductivity across the forecast interval challenges the forecast. A transition outside 90–120 K revises the quantitative model, rather than necessarily excluding all superconductivity.

## Next calculation with the highest value
Obtain published ordered H5 competitors and calculate balanced hydrogen grand potentials including configurational/vibrational contributions and uncertainty, then insertion/removal pathways and barriers. Do not compare per-atom entropies across different stoichiometries without balanced reactions. Dense-grid EPC validation of H6 remains useful, but phase access is the nearer bottleneck. CHGNet energies alone would not certify either Tc or hydride phase stability.
