# Ce heavy-fermions: ambient-pressure Tc enhancement audit

**Search cutoff: 10 October 2026.** Budget-limited primary-source searches covered CeCoIn5 strain, heterostructures and alloying, including explicit 2025–2026 searches. Dates below are publication dates, not crawler dates. This is not an exhaustive review. Scope excludes radioactive actinide materials. No numerical Tc prediction or candidate submission.

## Five strongest decision-relevant primary sources

**[1] Johnson, Zieve & Cooley, “Nonlinear effect of uniaxial pressure on superconductivity in CeCoIn5,” PRB 83, 144510; published 14 April 2011.** DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.83.144510 ; author manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4653 . C-axis uniaxial loading reaches **3.97 kbar (0.397 GPa)**; Tc peaks near **2 kbar (0.2 GPa)** and the transition broadens significantly. This demonstrates nonlinear mechanical tunability, not a scalable enhancement. The abstract discusses dimensionality; distinguishing hybridization changes from anisotropy and stress inhomogeneity requires further measurements. Peak Tc is not quoted because the checked publisher abstract does not quantify it. Applied stress is not an unloaded ambient-pressure result.

**[2] Gofryk et al., “Electronic Tuning and Uniform Superconductivity in CeCoIn5,” PRL 109, 186402; online 31 October 2012, issue 2 November 2012.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.186402 ; author manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.0672 . Pressure/codoping separates electronic tuning from pair breaking for Pt/Sn electron doping and Cd/Hg hole doping. Nominally nonmagnetic impurities produce unexpectedly weak d-wave pair breaking; hole dopants that induce moments are weaker pair breakers than electron dopants. Similar Pt/Sn effects despite different substitution sites challenge a strictly CeIn3-plane-only picture. **Alloying is neither pure chemical pressure nor pure disorder.** No above-parent ambient-pressure Tc gain is established by the evidence used here.

**[3] Naritsuka et al., “Tuning the Pairing Interaction in a d-Wave Superconductor by Paramagnons Injected through Interfaces,” PRL 120, 187002; published 4 May 2018.** DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.187002 . CeCoIn5/CeRhIn5 superlattices spatially separate superconductivity and antiferromagnetism. Hc2 analysis indicates extreme strong coupling when external pressure suppresses magnetic order. This supports interface tuning, but the key enhancement is **pressure-assisted and inferred from Hc2**, not demonstrated higher zero-field ambient-pressure Tc.

**[4] Asaba et al., “Evidence for a finite-momentum Cooper pair in tricolor d-wave superconducting superlattices,” Nature Communications 15, 3861; published 8 May 2024.** DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47875-4 . YbCoIn5(3)/CeCoIn5(8)/YbRhIn5(3), with thicknesses in unit cells and **30 repeats**, has **Tc = 0.83 K**, defined by resistance falling to **50%** of its normal-state value at onset; the bulk reference is **2.3 K**. Nonreciprocal transport supports high-field helical pairing, with vortex alternatives tested using orientation and bicolor controls. **Field robustness is not higher zero-field Tc.** Dimensionality-driven band/fluctuation changes are a proposed explanation for reduced Tc, not uniquely established.

**[5] Wang et al., “Asymmetric doping effects on the quantum critical compound CeRhIn5,” NPG Asia Materials 18, 10; published 19 March 2026.** DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41427-026-00639-6 . **5% Hg substitution on In sites** raises ambient-pressure TN from **3.8 to 5.1 K**. Transport to **24.1 GPa** finds **no superconductivity down to 0.3 K**. Suppressing magnetic order therefore does not guarantee superconductivity. Authors contrast magnetic-droplet/Kondo-screening changes with ordinary impurity pair breaking, arguing randomness alone is insufficient. This is a negative alloying control; Hg is toxic, so it is not a synthesis recommendation.

## Conclusions and competing interpretations

**Research priority, not a proven Tc ranking:** reversible clean strain first, controlled magnetic heterostructures second, random alloying principally as a tuning/control experiment [1–5]. Strain can modify hybridization and dimensionality but also broaden transitions [1]. Interfaces change magnetic coupling, proximity leakage, bands and inversion symmetry; Hc2 alone cannot establish higher Tc [3,4]. Alloying entangles electronic tuning with pair breaking, magnetic droplets and Kondo changes [2,5]. These sources do not support a numerical room-temperature extrapolation.

“Ambient pressure” must distinguish an externally strained, hydrostatically unpressurized device from an unloaded free-standing material. Neither external-pressure strong coupling [3] nor high-field helical pairing [4] establishes the latter.

## Falsifiable next step

Use one high-purity CeCoIn5 crystal with calibrated reversible strain, zero field and ambient hydrostatic pressure. Track resistive midpoint/width, ac susceptibility or calorimetry, residual resistivity and loading/unloading hysteresis. **Pass:** a reproducible increase of the bulk/shielding transition versus the same crystal relaxed, without increasing width or irreversible residual resistivity. **Fail:** only onset/Hc2 improves, the bulk transition is unchanged, or the effect follows cracking/hysteresis. Do not extrapolate a local response into a target Tc.

If successful, compare equal-thickness CeCoIn5/CeRhIn5 with nonmagnetic-spacer controls at matched measured strain. A zero-field Tc gain unique to magnetic interfaces and correlated with magnetic fluctuations supports injected pairing glue; reproduction by matched-strain nonmagnetic controls challenges that explanation [3].

**Confidence:** high in explicit measured numbers and publication dates; moderate in prioritization; low in extrapolated Tc gains. Search completeness is limited. No simulations, QPU jobs, or candidate submission.
