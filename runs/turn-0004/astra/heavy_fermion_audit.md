# Turn 4 independent heavy-fermion literature audit

Audit date: 2026-10-10. Scope: primary-paper evidence only; no candidate submission, no gamma-ceiling fit, and no new Tc model or extrapolation.

## Decision for coordination

**Do not promote this result as an ambient-pressure NEW material candidate.** The 2026 material is already-known **CeRu2**, measured as a nominally stoichiometric single crystal. Its unloaded transition is approximately **4.86 K (midpoint)**, with **onset near 5 K**. The novelty is mechanical tuning of an existing superconductor and its inferred gap structure, not a new composition, room-temperature superconductivity, or a retained stress-free phase. The small Tc maximum is measured with a uniaxial load near **0.13 GPa**; it must remain a loaded-state datum. No release-and-retain experiment, new chemical analogue with measured Tc, or ambient-pressure engineered-strain material is demonstrated. [1, Figs. 1–4, Results, Methods]

## CeRu2: composition and actual transition temperature

- **Composition:** Ce:Ru = **1:2 within elemental measurement accuracy**. Czochralski pulling from an arc-melted stoichiometric ingot; micro-XRF maps and point measurements on an approximately 2 × 2 mm surface region. This is CeRu2, not a newly specified CeRu(2−x), alloy, heterostructure or doped compound. The article describes a cubic Laves/pyrochlore structure with a Ru-based 3D kagome network. [1, Introduction; Methods: Single crystal growth, Sample characterization]
- **Best defensible unloaded/nominal zero-DC-field Tc:** **4.86 K midpoint**, per Fig. 1d caption; **approximately 5 K onset**, per Results. The stress series defines Tc by the **midpoint of the AC susceptibility transition**. These are different criteria, not contradictory discoveries of different Tc values. AC susceptibility necessarily uses an AC probe field, so this is not a mathematically exact zero-total-field measurement. The paper does not give a dedicated H→0 extrapolation for every stress condition, and the AC drive amplitude is not specified in the CeRu2 methods. Record “nominal zero DC field / AC susceptibility,” not an independently proven exact zero-field stress-dependent Tc. [1, Fig. 1d; Results: Stress effects on superconducting Tc; Uniaxial stress cell]
- The authors distinguish their approximately 5 K-onset crystal from earlier **Ru-deficient samples around 6 K**. That comparison does **not** supply a measured vacancy concentration or a new vacancy-engineered candidate in this experiment. [1, Results: Stress effects on superconducting Tc]
- **Tc under load:** approximately flat through **0.07 GPa**, a slight increase to a maximum near **0.13 GPa**, then suppression to approximately **4 K at 0.22 GPa**. The authors describe the latter as an approximately 16% decrease. The prose does not tabulate an exact maximum Tc; do not invent a high-precision optimum from the color map or interpret gap energies as Tc. The whole effect remains in the few-kelvin range. Hydrostatic pressure through **1.9 GPa** leaves Tc largely unchanged. [1, Abstract; Fig. 2; Results]

## Mechanical boundary conditions: stress is not strain

- **Uniaxial stress:** compression to approximately **0.22 GPa = 220 MPa = 2.2 kbar**, generated by a piezoelectric cell. Force calibration: approximately **31 N per μV**; maximum force approximately **560 N**; cross-sectional area **2.6 mm²**. Sample dimensions **7 × 4.4 × 0.6 mm³**, mounted with Stycast-2850 FT epoxy on a titanium holder. The small Tc optimum at **0.13 GPa** is therefore approximately **130 MPa / 1.3 kbar**, not an unloaded state. [1, Methods: Uniaxial stress cell]
- **Absolute strain is not quantitatively supplied.** Fig. 2c plots displacement-sensor voltage against force-sensor voltage, described as proportional to strain and stress. It does not establish a dimensionless lattice strain or percent strain without an additional calibration, gauge length and treatment of device/epoxy compliance. Fig. 2b labels its GPa horizontal axis “Strain”; dimensionally that axis is **stress**, not strain. Do not silently turn 0.13 GPa into 0.13% strain. [1, Fig. 2b–c; Methods]
- **Orientation inconsistency:** Fig. 1a caption says compression **within the (111) kagome plane**, with unknown in-plane direction. Methods say [111] is normal to the exposed surface and then conclude stress is **along [111]**, which is normal to that plane. The reported cross-section and platelet geometry are compatible with in-plane loading, but that is an inference, not a resolution of the wording conflict. Seek author clarification before any direction-specific elastic calculation. [1, Fig. 1a; Methods: Sample characterization, Uniaxial stress cell; peer-review author response]
- **Hydrostatic loading:** piston-cylinder apparatus with **Daphne 7373**, calibrated by an indium superconducting transition; maximum published pressure **1.9 GPa**. This is externally maintained loading, not ambient-pressure synthesis. [1, Methods: Hydrostatic pressure cell]

## Bulk versus filamentary evidence

**Assessment: substantial magnetic/microscopic evidence for superconductivity in the sampled crystal, not a transport-only filamentary claim; not a quantified 100%-volume proof at every stress.** [1]

- The unloaded sample has a resistive superconducting transition and AC diamagnetic response. Under uniaxial stress, susceptibility tracks the transition directly. Transverse-field μSR sees vortex-state broadening and a diamagnetic field shift, and its temperature dependence is analyzed as superfluid density. This combination is much stronger than an isolated resistive onset, which could originate from a small percolating path. [1, Figs. 1–3; Results]
- The μSR superconducting measurements use **30 mT**, both for uniaxial stress (gap data through **0.18 GPa**) and hydrostatic pressure (through **1.9 GPa**). They probe a finite-field vortex state, not zero-field superconducting transitions. The Tc point at **0.22 GPa** is from the susceptibility series; do not claim μSR gap characterization extends to that stress. [1, Results: Stress effects on superfluid density and gap structure; Hydrostatic pressure]
- Approximately **40% of incoming muons stop in the sample** in either loading setup. This is a **beam stopping fraction**, not a 40% superconducting volume fraction. Background components are separately treated. The paper does not provide a calibrated absolute shielding/Meissner fraction or a thermodynamic heat-capacity anomaly under every load. Thus neither “filamentary only” nor “proved 100% bulk at all stresses” is warranted. [1, Methods: loading cells; μSR analysis]

## CeCoIn5 cross-check: useful caution, no new candidate

Composition is the known **CeCoIn5** crystal, grown from In flux, not a newly designed compound. Its nominal unloaded low-field onset is about **2.3 K** (roughly 2.32 K on Fig. 3's scale); the loaded onset changes only by tens of millikelvin, with a maximum near **2 kbar = 0.20 GPa**. Do not assign extra digits from the figure. Compression is explicitly **along the tetragonal c axis**, reaching **3.97 kbar = 0.397 GPa**. No measured percent lattice strain is reported. [2, Introduction, Methods; Figs. 2–3]

The onset is defined as the first deviation of susceptibility from its normal-state value. Meanwhile, the transition **midpoint moves downward** because its width grows from **112 mK at nominal zero load to 270 mK at 3.97 kbar**. The AC field is about **0.3 gauss**, with no DC field stated. A possible mounting/thermal-contraction pressure offset is not included in the quoted applied pressure; comparison outside the cell suggests an offset less than **0.2 kbar**. [2, Methods; Results; Figs. 2–4]

The effect is reversible over pressure sweeps, with retained crystal structure afterward; that is evidence for load tuning, not a newly synthesized retained-enhancement phase. The stress experiment provides susceptibility rather than transport-only evidence, but does not independently measure calorimetric volume fraction or prove the elevated onset represents a uniform-volume higher-Tc state. **CeRu2 midpoint values and CeCoIn5 onset values must not be pooled without criterion labels.** [2, Methods; Results]

## Extrapolation and interpretation traps

1. **Ambient gas pressure ≠ mechanically unloaded.** Maintaining a uniaxial compressive load while the surrounding environment is at atmospheric pressure does not make the enhanced datum a stress-free ambient-pressure candidate. No chemical-pressure analogue or retained unloaded strain state is demonstrated here. [1–2; audit judgment]
2. **Do not extrapolate a small dome or initial slope to room temperature.** Both curves turn over; CeRu2 ultimately loses Tc. CeCoIn5's reported initial slope **17 mK/kbar** is local and onset-based, not a scalable enhancement law. No gamma-ceiling fit was performed. [1, Fig. 2; 2, Results]
3. **Gap evolution is not a high-Tc discovery.** CeRu2's anisotropic-to-isotropic nodeless crossover and hydrostatic-pressure nodal signature do not demonstrate large Tc enhancement. At 1.9 GPa, linear low-temperature superfluid density is consistent with nodes, but the authors explicitly say it need not establish d-wave symmetry. [1, Results; Tables 1–2]
4. **Published anisotropy-ratio wording is internally inconsistent.** The text says Δmin/Δmax “vanishes” on becoming isotropic; physical isotropy requires that ratio to approach **1**, while zero means a vanishing minimum gap. Table 1 omits Δmin at higher stresses. Interpret the intended result as reduced anisotropy, not new nodes under uniaxial load. [1, Stress effects on superfluid density and gap structure; Table 1; audit reasoning]
5. **Linear force-displacement response is not loaded-state crystallographic proof.** The authors infer absence of a structural transition from an elastic response. This alone neither proves all space-group properties are unchanged nor excludes weak symmetry lowering under uniaxial compression. [1, Fig. 2c; audit judgment]
6. **Flat-band motion is a proposed mechanism, not measured stress-resolved ARPES or an ab initio candidate prediction.** Do not assign a new composition or Tc from the hypothesized DOS shift. The paper itself leaves the collective Ce/Ru contributions unresolved. [1, Discussion]
7. **T* ≈ 40 K is a normal-state magnetic/electronic anomaly, not superconducting Tc.** The associated 9 T magnetotransport and previous TRS-breaking observations must not be relabeled as zero-field superconductivity. [1, Fig. 1d–e; Results]
8. **Version control matters:** the related arXiv abstract gives hydrostatic pressure up to **2.2 GPa**, whereas the publisher article, Table 2 and methods give **1.9 GPa**. Use the published value for this audit. The 2026 article is not a 2025 discovery simply because its preprint identifier starts with 2507. [1; arXiv:2507.09779 abstract]
9. **Heavy-fermion/kagome terminology is not a universal quantitative Tc scale.** The paper discusses hybridized itinerant Ce-4f states and proposes a heavy-fermion perspective; this is not evidence that every Ce compound shares CeCoIn5's mechanism or that a cross-family fit validates a novel material. [1, Introduction, Conclusion; audit judgment]

## Confidence and next action

**High confidence** in composition, few-kelvin baseline Tc, imposed-load ranges, finite-field μSR distinction, and the decision that these papers do not justify an ambient-pressure NEW candidate. **Moderate confidence** in precise direction-dependent interpretation and the quantitative uniform superconducting volume under each load, because the orientation text conflicts and absolute volume calibration is absent. Exact peak Tc, percent strain, and a dedicated zero-field extrapolation are **not established** by the tabulated record; raw data are available from the CeRu2 authors on request.

Recommended bookkeeping: retain CeRu2 and CeCoIn5 as **known-material mechanical-tuning references**, with separate fields for composition, Tc criterion, DC/AC probe field, stress tensor/orientation, and applied load. A new candidate would require a specified new composition or stable unloaded engineered structure plus independent evidence; these papers do not provide it. No candidate was submitted.
