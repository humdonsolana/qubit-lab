Study on fluctuations of interface-enhanced superconductivity in ultrathin
FeSe/SrTiO3 by the Nernst effect
Tomoki Kobayashi, Ryo Ogawa, and Atsutaka Maeda
Department of Basic Science, The University of Tokyo,
3-8-1 Komaba, Meguro-ku, Tokyo 153-8902, Japan
(Dated: September 23, 2025)
Ultrathin FeSe films on SrTiO3 substrate show interface-enhanced superconductivity. However,
how the superconductivity is established including superconducting fluctuations remains unclear.
This study investigates the Nernst effect, which is sensitive to superconducting fluctuations, in
ultrathin FeSe films on SrTiO3. Temperature dependence of Nernst signals in the normal state is
similar to bulk FeSe, suggesting that the electrons of SrTiO3 are transferred only to a few layers
near the FeSe/SrTiO3 interface. The Nernst effect caused by SC fluctuations was observed only
belowT∼1.2T onsetc within our measurement resolution, which is similar to other Fe chalcogenide
systems. Our results suggest that the pseudogap in monolayer FeSe/STO possibly originates in
other electronic states rather than superconductivity.
I. INTRODUCTION
Ultrathin FeSe/SrTiO3 (STO) has attracted consider-
able attention owing to its higher superconducting tran-
sition temperature (T c) compared to that of its bulk
form[1, 2]. The FeSe/STO interface is repotedly plays
a crucial role in increasing theTc. Charge transfer from
STO[3–5] and possible electron-phonon coupling between
electrons in FeSe and phonons of STO[6–8] have been
studied as possible mechanisms causingTc enhancement.
While the ultrathin FeSe/STO had been fabricated only
by molecular beam epitaxy (MBE), a similar supercon-
ductivity was recently achieved by pulsed laser deposi-
tion (PLD)[9, 10]. Similar to MBE-grown films, in PLD-
grown films, the electrons in STO are transferred to FeSe,
and the FeSe/STO interface is critical for achieving in-
creasedT c. Even though the thicknesses of these films
are not monolayer, the measurement results of the upper
critical field indicated confinement of superconductivity
to a monolayer or a few layers near the FeSe/STO inter-
face [11].
In monolayer FeSe/STO grown using MBE, the
achievedT c was reported to be 65 K due to the
gap opening at the Fermi level, as observed using
angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES)[2].
Feng group reported the establishment of supercon-
ductivity below 60–65 K, which correspond to gap-
opening temperatures, usingµSR[12] and diamagnetism
measurements[13]. However, nootherstudieshaveyetre-
ported reliable evidence of superconductivity at 65 K us-
ing transport and diamagnetism measurements. Regard-
less of in-situ[14, 15]or ex-situ[1] measurements, a resis-
tive transition with onsetTc (T onsetc )∼40 K and zero re-
sistivity belowT zeroc <29 K[1, 14, 15] have been reported
with an exception of a singular report[16]. The observed
gap at approximately 65 K may be attributed to strong
superconducting (SC) fluctuations[14, 15]. However, the
gap around the Fermi level is also observed in other or-
deredstatesincludingthepseudogapphaseincuprate[17]
and charge density wave in kagome superconductors[18].
Therefore, further studies are needed to understand the
relation between the gap opening temperature,T onsetc ,
andT zeroc .
One of the probes sensitive to SC fluctuations is the
Nernst effect, which is the generation of a transverse elec-
tric field by a longitudinal thermal gradient. Nernst ef-
fect was originally studied for vortex motion induced by
the thermal gradient[19, 20]. In the last few decades,
the Nernst effect by SC fluctuations has been reported.
The SC fluctuations can be categorized to amplitude
fluctuations, which is interpreted by short-lived cooper
pairs, and phase fluctuations where proliferated vortices
plays a role. The Nernst effect by amplitude fluctu-
atinos was reported for NbSi amorphous thin film [21]
at first. Subsequently, it was also observed in cuprate
[22–24] and other amorphous or crystalline conventional
superconductor[25, 26]. The Nernst effect caused by
phase fluctuations have been reported in hole-doped
cuprate[27], although it is still under debated[23]. Fea-
tures of the Nernst effect caused by amplitude or phase
fluctuations have been studied theoretically[28–30], al-
lowing us to investigate the nature of these SC fluctua-
tions.
In this study, we investigated the Nernst effect in ul-
trathin FeSe films on STO. Temperature dependence of
Nernst signals in the normal state is similar to bulk FeSe,
suggesting that the electrons of STO are transferred only
to a few layers near the FeSe/STO interface. The Nernst
effect caused by SC fluctuations was observed only below
T∼1.2T onsetc within our measurement resolution, which
is similar to those observed in other Fe chalcogenide sys-
tems. The comparison to theories suggests that the ob-
served Nernst effect caused by the SC fluctuations orig-
inates from amplitude fluctuations of a superconducting
parameter. Our results suggest that the pseudogap in
monolayer FeSe/STO may originate in electronic states
other than superconductivity.

FeSefilmswithHallbargeometryweregrownonatom-
ically flat STO (100) substrates with the step-terrace

The film thicknesses of the grown films were estimated
using FeSe(001) reflections[9]. The thicknesses of FeSe
films in this study were approximately 2.5 and 5 nm,
which correspond to 4–5 and 9 layers. Resistance mea-

using a physical property measurement system (PPMS).
Thermoelectric measurements were performed with the
one-heater-two-thermometer configuration (Fig. 1(a)).
The temperature gradient∇Twas measured with two
Cernox thermometers attached to the samples via Cu

Moreover, phosphor bronze wires were used for voltage
measurements. Because the Si capping layer is insulat-
ing, we scratched the contact pads before attaching the
wires to the pads to ensure a direct electrical contact
with FeSe. The measurement was performed with fixed

IV. CONCLUSION
In conclusion, we investigated the Nernst effect in ul-
trathin FeSe films on STO. The temperature dependence
of Nernst signals in the normal state is similar to that ob-
servedforbulkFeSe, suggestingthattheelectronsofSTO
are transferred only to a few layers near the FeSe/STO
interface, whichisconsistentwiththefindingsofprevious
reports[5, 11]. Increase ofα2Dxy and variation of magnetic
fielddependenceisobservedbelowT ∗ ∼1.2T onsetc within
our measurement resolution, which is similar to other Fe-
chalcogenide systems. A comparison to theories suggests
that the observed Nenrst effect by the SC fluctuations
originates from amplitude fluctuations of the supercon-
ducting parameter. Our obtained results suggest that
the origin of the pseudogap in monolayer FeSe/STO may
originate in other electronic states rather than supercon-
ductivity.
